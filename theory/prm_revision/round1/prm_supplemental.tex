\section{Data provenance}

The figures and tables in this Supplemental Material cover the same
103 cation--oxygen species analyzed in the main text, spanning $s$-,
$p$-, $d$-, and $f$-block cations.

Source structures are DFT-relaxed entries from the Materials Project.
For each structure, bond strengths were obtained by solving the
valence-sum and loop constraints on the cation--oxygen bond graph,
and $(R_0,B)$ were determined by linear least squares.  For
rank-deficient bond graphs (fewer independent bonds than parameters),
an anion-centered reparameterization was used.  Any remaining
ill-conditioned cases were resolved with the constrained nonlinear
procedure of Ref.~\citenum{Li2025}, with $R_0$ bounds $(-10,10)$~\AA.
Coordination numbers were determined from the bond graph.

The DFT-centroid comparison in Fig.~2 of the main text uses ten $s$-block oxides in
panel~a (Li$_2$O, Na$_2$O, K$_2$O, Rb$_2$O, Cs$_2$O, BeO, MgO, CaO,
SrO, and BaO) and 25 non-$s$-block oxides in panel~b: Al$_2$O$_3$,
SiO$_2$, GeO$_2$, SnO$_2$, PbO, TiO$_2$, V$_2$O$_5$, CoO, NiO, CuO,
ZnO, ZrO$_2$, MoO$_3$, PdO, Ag$_2$O, HfO$_2$, Ta$_2$O$_5$, Au$_2$O$_3$,
La$_2$O$_3$, Gd$_2$O$_3$, Yb$_2$O$_3$, B$_2$O$_3$, P$_2$O$_5$,
As$_2$O$_3$, and Sb$_2$O$_3$.  The panel-$b$ regression is defined
a priori as a test of the Yukawa screening picture only for
ground-state binary oxides whose cation environments approximate an
isotropic first-shell ionic screening cloud.  It therefore uses the 21
filled-symbol oxides and excludes B$_2$O$_3$, P$_2$O$_5$,
As$_2$O$_3$, and Au$_2$O$_3$, whose ground-state structures are dominated by
directional, non-isotropic bonding motifs.  Those four compounds are shown
as open symbols.  The 21 oxides shown as filled symbols in main-text Fig.~2(b)
are the 25 listed above minus the four directional cases just named.
No further data-driven exclusion was applied beyond this fixed list.  The excluded oxides are the cases in this benchmark whose
ground-state cation environments are not compact first-shell ionic
polyhedra but directional or terminal-oxyanion motifs, so a single
shell centroid $m_i$ is not an adequate descriptor of the screening
cloud.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Coordination-number-resolved $B$--$R_0$ fit lines}{Coordination-number-resolved B-R0 fit lines}}

Figures~\ref{fig:group1_fits}--\ref{fig:fblock_fits_2} show the
$n$-resolved $B$ vs.\ $R_0$ fit lines for every species in the
103-cation set, grouped by block: Group~1 (Fig.~\ref{fig:group1_fits}),
Group~2 (Fig.~\ref{fig:group2_fits}), $3d$/$4d$/$5d$ transition metals
(Figs.~\ref{fig:dblock_fits_1}--\ref{fig:dblock_fits_8}), $p$-block
(Figs.~\ref{fig:pblock_fits_1}--\ref{fig:pblock_fits_3}), and $f$-block
lanthanides plus actinides
(Figs.~\ref{fig:fblock_fits_1}--\ref{fig:fblock_fits_2}).  Each
caption quotes the species panels of its page in reading order, either
in full or as a first--last range.  Within each block, panels are
ordered alphabetically by element symbol and oxidation state.  Within each
coordination shell, the fitted parameters lie on a linear family
$B=\beta^{(n)}R_0+\beta_0^{(n)}$, and the lines converge toward
cation-specific characteristic pairs $(R_0^*,B^*)$ as described in the
main text.  Together, these atlas pages display every coordination-resolved
fit that contributes to the pole form $\beta=1/\ln(z/n)$ shown in
Fig.~1 of the main text.

\paragraph*{Line-fitting procedure.}
Each DFT-relaxed structure contributes one independently fitted
$(R_0,B)$ pair.  With the solved $S_{ij}$ as data, the model
$R_{ij}=R_0-B\ln S_{ij}$ is linear in both parameters, which are
determined jointly by ordinary least squares (OLS) over the
structure's cation--oxygen bonds.  $R_0$ is fixed and $B$ re-optimized
only in the rare bound-projection fallback, when the unconstrained
$R_0$ falls outside the pipeline's $R_0$ bounds.  A structure enters
coordination class $n$ only when its cation sites share an unambiguous
oxygen coordination number in the bond graph.  Mixed-coordination
structures are left unbinned.  For each species and coordination class
with at least five structures, the line $B=\beta^{(n)}R_0+\beta_0^{(n)}$
is fitted both by OLS and by random sample consensus (RANSAC), with
residual threshold equal to twice the median absolute OLS residual and
floored at $0.01$~\AA, and the RANSAC line is used whenever available.
The reported slope and intercept come from an OLS refit on the RANSAC
inlier set, while their standard errors and confidence intervals come
from a 400-resample bootstrap over that inlier set.  The population
$N_n$ of a line is the number of inlier structures in this final
refit.  It supplies the weights $w=\max(N_n,1)$ used in the
goodness-of-fit metrics of main-text Fig.~1 and in the
characteristic-pair intersections below.  Lines enter Fig.~1 of the
main text only if they retain at least four inliers, have slope
standard error at most $5$, and have $R^2\ge 0.3$.

\paragraph*{Sign census of the per-shell fits.}
Of the 79{,}009 fitted shells in the corpus, 63{,}783 carry a
single-valence oxidation-state assignment together with defined $B$,
$z$, and $n$, and 16.4\% of these return $B<0$ (the main text's count
of 7{,}565 shells at $z=n$ additionally includes mixed-valence
records).  The $B<0$ fraction is 20.4\% for $z<n$, 5.8\% for $z=n$,
and 10.2\% for $z>n$.  The negative sign is dominated by
near-degenerate shells whose fitted $B$ is barely constrained.  The
median bond-length range of the $B<0$ shells is $0.059$~\AA{},
roughly half the $0.111$~\AA{} of the $B>0$ shells, and 47\% of
$B<0$ shells have ranges within $0.05$~\AA{} compared with 29\% of
$B>0$ shells.  The chemically systematic $B<0$ fractions concentrate
in square-planar and linear late transition metals (47\% for
Pd$^{2+}$, 43\% for Au$^{3+}$, 37\% for Cu$^{1+}$) and in large,
soft cations in high, irregular coordination (51\% for Eu$^{2+}$,
40\% for Rb$^{+}$, 31\% for Sr$^{2+}$), consistent with directional
bonding or ambiguous coordination producing anisotropic flux
capture.  Resolved against the band gap of the host structure, the
$B<0$ fraction is 20\% among clean fits in metallic hosts versus
12\% across gapped hosts (Fig.~\ref{fig:gap_sign_census}),
consistent with over-screening where free carriers are present.  The
census is reproduced by
\texttt{analysis/scripts/bv\_sign\_census.py} in the project
repository \cite{YukawaScreeningRepo}.

\paragraph*{Weighting scheme for the goodness-of-fit metrics.}
The weighted $R^2$ and mean absolute error reported with Fig.~1 of the
main text use weights $w_i=\max(N_i,1)$, where $N_i$ is the number of
DFT-relaxed structures retained as inliers in the final linear
$B$--$R_0$ fit at species $i$ and coordination $n$ (see the
line-fitting procedure above).  This gives more populous (species,
$n$) cells more leverage, in proportion to the number of independent
structures backing each fitted slope $\beta_i$.  Letting
$\hat\beta_i=1/\ln(z_i/n)$ be the parameter-free closed-form prediction
and $\bar\beta_w=\sum_i w_i\beta_i/\sum_i w_i$, the reported metrics are
\[
R^2_w
= 1 - \frac{\sum_i w_i\,(\beta_i-\hat\beta_i)^2}
            {\sum_i w_i\,(\beta_i-\bar\beta_w)^2},
\qquad
\mathrm{MAE}_w
= \frac{\sum_i w_i\,|\beta_i-\hat\beta_i|}{\sum_i w_i}.
\]
Pooling 68 species at $n=4$ ($\sum w=13{,}370$) with 82 species at
$n=6$ ($\sum w=17{,}162$) -- the $z\ne n$ subset shown in Fig.~1 --
gives $R^2_w=0.986$ and $\mathrm{MAE}_w=0.15$ for the parameter-free
closed form $\beta=1/\ln(z/n)$.  The $n=4$ panel alone gives
$R^2_w=0.973$ with $\mathrm{MAE}_w=0.23$, and the $n=6$ panel gives
$R^2_w=0.979$ with $\mathrm{MAE}_w=0.089$.

\paragraph*{Slope standard errors.}
Each fitted slope carries a standard error $\sigma_\beta$ from the
400-resample bootstrap described in the line-fitting procedure above.
Across the 150 slopes the median relative standard error is $0.19\%$
and the 90th percentile is $3.9\%$, roughly two orders of magnitude
below the across-species spread at the formal charges flanking the
pole.  The per-species slopes are drawn with these error bars in
Fig.~\ref{fig:pole_species}.

\paragraph*{Exclusion of $z=n$ species from main-text Fig.~1.}
Species at $z=n$ (U$^{4+}$, Mn$^{4+}$, Ti$^{4+}$, V$^{4+}$, Zr$^{4+}$,
Ir$^{4+}$, P$^{4+}$, Pb$^{4+}$, and Te$^{4+}$ for $n=4$, and Mo$^{6+}$ and
W$^{6+}$ for $n=6$) are omitted from
Fig.~1 of the main text.  At $z=n$ the valence-sum rule fixes the mean
bond valence at unity but not each $S_{ij}$ individually.  For a
degenerate shell with equal bond lengths the rule is satisfied for any
$B$ with $R_0=\bar R$, leaving the $B$--$R_0$ slope $\beta$ formally
unconstrained.  For nearly degenerate shells the $S_{ij}$ differ and
$B$ is finite, but the fit is ill-conditioned because the intercept
ceases to constrain the softness at $z=n$ (see the screening-collapse
discussion in the main text).  Accordingly, the fits exhibit very
large variance (see the
$z=n$ panels in
Figs.~\ref{fig:dblock_fits_1}--\ref{fig:dblock_fits_8} and
\ref{fig:pblock_fits_1}--\ref{fig:fblock_fits_2}, where the
coordination-resolved lines have widely scattered slopes and
intercepts).  The closed-form expression $\beta=1/\ln(z/n)$ correctly
diverges at $z=n$, and the divergence itself is a quantitative
prediction of the Yukawa screening picture.  We plot only $z\ne n$
species so that finite, well-conditioned fitted slopes are not
visually conflated with the formal pole.  Main-text Fig.~1 therefore
shows the
94 species (150 fitted slopes) for which $z\ne n$ at $n=4$ or $n=6$.
Six species (Cl$^{1-}$, C$^{4+}$, Hg$^{1+}$, H$^{1+}$, N$^{3+}$,
S$^{4+}$) lack a fittable line at either $n=4$ or $n=6$, P$^{4+}$
lies exactly at the $n=4$ pole, and Te$^{6+}$ and U$^{6+}$ fall
below the $R^2\ge 0.3$ acceptance threshold at $n=6$.

\section{Characteristic pairs and bond-length identity}

Figure~\ref{fig:r0star_bstar} shows the characteristic pairs
$(R_0^*,B^*)$ for Group~1 (including H$^+$), Group~2, and the
trivalent lanthanides.

For each species and coordination shell $n$, the per-structure
$(R_0,B)$ pairs define a line $B=\beta^{(n)}R_0+\beta_0^{(n)}$, fitted
by the robust procedure described above.  The characteristic pair is
the weighted least-spread intersection of those $n$-resolved lines.
All available coordination lines enter, with no $R^2$ screening at
this stage.  If the final line fit for shell $n$ retains $N_n$ inlier
structures, we assign weight $w_n=N_n$ and define the weighted means
\[
\bar\beta=\frac{\sum_n w_n\beta^{(n)}}{\sum_n w_n},
\qquad
\bar\beta_0=\frac{\sum_n w_n\beta_0^{(n)}}{\sum_n w_n}.
\]
The characteristic abscissa is then
\[
R_0^*=
-\frac{\sum_n w_n\bigl(\beta^{(n)}-\bar\beta\bigr)
\bigl(\beta_0^{(n)}-\bar\beta_0\bigr)}
{\sum_n w_n\bigl(\beta^{(n)}-\bar\beta\bigr)^2},
\]
which is the point that minimizes the weighted variance of the
line-by-line values $B_n(R_0)=\beta^{(n)}R_0+\beta_0^{(n)}$.  The
characteristic ordinate and its spread are
\[
B^*=
\frac{\sum_n w_n B_n(R_0^*)}{\sum_n w_n},
\qquad
\sigma_B=
\left[
\frac{\sum_n w_n\bigl(B_n(R_0^*)-B^*\bigr)^2}
{\sum_n w_n}
\right]^{1/2}.
\]
The tabulated $n_{\mathrm{CN}}$ counts how many distinct coordination
shells contribute to that intersection, while the weights entering the
intersection are the fitted structure counts in those shells.

\paragraph*{Near-coincidence of same-branch lines.}
For many species the $n$-resolved lines appear nearly coincident.
This is expected from the pole form.  Subtracting two predicted
slopes gives
\[
\beta^{(n_2)}-\beta^{(n_1)}
=\frac{\ln(n_2/n_1)}{\ln(z/n_1)\,\ln(z/n_2)}.
\]
The numerator involves only the coordination ratio and is small for
neighboring shells.  When $z$ lies on the same side of both
coordination numbers and well away from each, the denominator is of
order unity, so same-branch lines are nearly parallel over the
plotted windows and their pairwise crossing is delicate.  As $z$
approaches either coordination number the corresponding line steepens
toward the pole, the slope difference grows, and the crossing
sharpens.  When instead $n_1<z<n_2$ the two slopes have opposite
signs and the crossing is sharp.  Equation~(7) of the main text makes
the conditioning explicit.  The crossing ordinate is the shell
dilation $[\bar R^{(n_2)}-\bar R^{(n_1)}]/\ln(n_2/n_1)$, so the
intersection is well constrained when the corpus resolves the
dilation of the shell with coordination number and only weakly
constrained when it does not.  The characteristic pair is therefore
never computed from a single pair of lines.  The weighted
least-spread construction above pools every available coordination
line, weights each by its fitted structure count, and reports the
spread $\sigma_B$ at $R_0^*$ as the conditioning diagnostic in
Table~\ref{tab:lambda_star}.  Across the 85 species with three or
more lines the median $\sigma_B/B^*$ is $5.8\%$, and 62 fall below
$10\%$.  The weakest case is Ag$^{1+}$, whose near-zero
$B^*=0.011$~\AA{} inflates the relative spread.  The 18 species with
only two lines have $\sigma_B=0$ by construction and are marked
provisional.

\section{Comparison with published softness parameters}

The characteristic softness $B^*$ of Eq.~(7) of the main text is a
single species-level constant obtained by aggregating per-structure
fits across the corpus, and is therefore directly comparable to
published bond-valence parameter sets, each of which assigns one
softness per cation--anion pair by pooling all structures containing
that pair into a single fit.  Brown and Altermatt fix $B=0.37$~\AA{} for every
pair and fit only $R_0$ \cite{Brown1985}.  The softBV
parameterization fits one $b$ per pair from pooled structure data
with coordination-dependent cutoffs \cite{Adams2001,Chen2017}, and
Gagn\'e and Hawthorne refit both parameters per pair from a large
pooled corpus \cite{Gagn2015}.  Species are matched to the published
sets by element and oxidation state.

\paragraph*{Distribution-level agreement.}
Across the 101 screening-branch species, $B^*$ has mean
$0.38\pm0.15$~\AA, median $0.37$~\AA, interquartile range
$0.31$--$0.44$~\AA, and full range $0.004$--$0.918$~\AA, clustering
near the conventional assignment $B=0.37$~\AA{} \cite{Brown1985}.  The softBV set spans $0.34$--$0.62$~\AA{} over
155 cation--oxygen pairs (mean $0.45\pm0.05$~\AA), and the
Gagn\'e--Hawthorne set spans $0.25$--$0.66$~\AA{} over 135 pairs
(mean $0.40\pm0.06$~\AA).  On matched species the mean offset of
$B^*$ from the Gagn\'e--Hawthorne values is $-0.01$~\AA{} (92
species, mean absolute difference $0.11$~\AA) and from softBV
$-0.06$~\AA{} (99 species, mean absolute difference $0.13$~\AA).
The distribution is therefore consistent with the spread determined
by flexible per-pair fitting \cite{Gagn2015} while sitting lower
than the values deployed by softBV \cite{Adams2001,Chen2017}.
The extreme $B^*$ tails are the weakly conditioned intersections
flagged in Table~\ref{tab:lambda_star}.  Restricting to the
well-conditioned species, with at least three coordination lines and
$\sigma_B/B^*\le 20\%$, leaves 78 species with mean
$0.39\pm0.12$~\AA, median $0.37$~\AA, and range
$0.14$--$0.79$~\AA.

\paragraph*{Species-level decorrelation.}
Species by species, $B^*$ is uncorrelated with either published set
($r=0.09$ against softBV and $r=-0.01$ against
Gagn\'e--Hawthorne, essentially unchanged on the well-conditioned
subset).  This is not specific to the present method.  The two
published sets correlate only weakly with each other ($r=0.26$, mean
absolute difference $0.065$~\AA{} over 130 common pairs), because a
pooled per-pair fit selects one point in the shallow $(R_0,B)$
valley documented by Gagn\'e and Hawthorne \cite{Gagn2015}, and
where that point falls depends on which coordination numbers and
bonding environments dominate the training corpus.  The
per-material fits used here resolve the valley instead of averaging
over it.  At fixed species and coordination number the
per-structure $(R_0,B)$ pairs fall on the lines of slope
$1/\ln(z/n)$ shown in the atlas above, so the covariance that
pooled fitting suppresses carries the physical content, and $B^*$
is defined geometrically as the least-spread intersection of those
lines rather than as a corpus average.  The two estimands therefore
agree in scale and center but need not agree pair by pair.

\section{\texorpdfstring{$s$-block screening-centroid controls}{s-block screening-centroid controls}}

For the ten Group~1/Group~2 ground-state binary oxides used in Fig.~2 of the
main text, the DFT screening centroid $r_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is compared
against three linear descriptors: the bond-valence shell centroid $m_i$,
the fitted bond-valence intercept $R_0$, and the Shannon cation radius.
Leave-one-out mean absolute errors are $0.006$~\AA\ for $m_i$,
$0.078$~\AA\ for $R_0$, and $0.095$~\AA\ for the Shannon-radius control,
showing that the $r_{\mathrm{eff}}$--$m_i$ collapse is substantially
sharper than a generic monotone size trend.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{thomas_fermi_reff_null_models_si.png}
\caption[Null-model check for the ten binary oxides.]{Leave-one-out null-model check for the ten Group~1/Group~2
binary oxides used in Fig.~2 of the main text.  Bars report the mean
absolute error of linear regressions against the bond-valence shell
centroid $m_i$, the fitted intercept $R_0$, and the Shannon cation radius.}
\label{fig:sblock_controls}

Each oxide contributes one point $(m_i,r_{\mathrm{eff}})$ to
main-text Fig.~2.  For a given oxide, the bond-valence shell centroid is computed
from the same fitted $(R_0,B)$ and coordination number $n$ used for its
bond-valence record, taking the $n$ shortest M--O distances in the
ground-state structure and forming
\[
p_{ij}=\frac{e^{-R_{ij}/B}}{\sum_{k=1}^n e^{-R_{ik}/B}},
\qquad
m_i=\sum_{j=1}^n p_{ij}R_{ij}.
\]
The DFT-side centroid is then built bond by bond over all oxygen
neighbors with $R_{ij}\le 3.5$~\AA.  Each such bond contributes one
oxygen-side path centroid
$r_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(\mathrm{path})}$, and the oxide-level
$r_{\mathrm{eff}}$ plotted in main-text Fig.~2 is the simple arithmetic mean of
those finite bond-path centroids.

The offset in the main-text regression is quantified directly from that same
discrete bond-path construction.  Defining the oxygen-side pre-cusp valence centroid
shift by
\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{O,val}}:=\langle R_{ij}-r_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(\mathrm{path})}\rangle,
\]
let $\{r_g,\rho_g\}_{g=1}^{200}$ denote the total-density samples on the
nearest-image straight M--O segment at 200 uniformly spaced points.  The
bond critical-point proxy is the interior density minimum on the interval
$0.15R_{ij}<r<0.85R_{ij}$.  Denote its location and value by
$r_{\mathrm{BCP}}$ and $\rho_{\mathrm{BCP}}$.  The oxygen-side pre-cusp
window is the subset of sampled points with
$r_{\mathrm{BCP}}\le r_g\le 0.93R_{ij}$, finite $\rho_g$, and positive
finite Thomas--Fermi screening length $\lambda_{\mathrm{TF}}(\rho_g)$.
The Thomas--Fermi length enters here only as a positivity filter on the
sample window and does not appear in the centroid weights or in any of
the regression results.  The \texttt{thomas\_fermi} tag in the
main-text Fig.~2 file label reflects only the screening interpretation of
$r_{\mathrm{eff}}$, not its construction.
On that discrete window we use the excess-density weights
$w_g=\max[\rho_g-\rho_{\mathrm{BCP}},0]$.  In the rare case that these
clipped weights vanish identically, we use $w_g=\rho_g$ instead.  Thus
\[
r_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(\mathrm{path})}=
\frac{\sum_g w_g r_g}{\sum_g w_g},
\]
and the oxide-level centroid used in main-text Fig.~2 is the arithmetic mean of
$r_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(\mathrm{path})}$ over those first-shell M--O bonds.
No spline fitting, path relaxation, or topological bond-path search is
introduced beyond this fixed straight-segment sampling rule.  With that
definition, the ten-oxide $s$-block shift fit is
$\Delta_{\mathrm{O,val}} = 0.0613\,m_i + 0.2394$~\AA\
($R^2=0.963$), with mean $0.3910\pm0.0269$~\AA.  Equivalently,
$r_{\mathrm{eff}}=0.9387\,m_i-0.2394$~\AA, whose rounded form is the
main-text relation.  The intercept is therefore the zero-radius limit of
a directly measured oxygen-side screening shift, not an unconstrained
empirical parameter.

\paragraph*{Species-level screening length from the characteristic pair.}
The shell-level Yukawa inversion in the main text is written in terms of the
representative radius $R'$, chosen operationally as the Gibbs shell
centroid $m_i$.  To pass from that shell-level relation to a species-level
constant, we evaluate the inversion at the characteristic fixed point
$(R_0^*,B^*)$ on the characteristic reference shell defined by $m_i=R_0^*$.
Using the exact identity
\[
R_0 = m_i + B(\ln q_i - H_i),
\]
this reference-shell condition is equivalent at the fixed point to
$H_i=\ln q_i$, so the representative radius entering the Yukawa inversion is
$R'=R_0^*$.  Substituting $B=B^*$ and $R'=R_0^*$ into the positive screening
branch therefore gives
\[
\lambda^*=\frac{-R_0^*+\sqrt{R_0^{*2}+4B^*R_0^*}}{2},
\]
which is the species-level screening length reported in
Table~\ref{tab:lambda_star}.  For the two anti-screening species
($B^*<0$) the same branch-continuous root is real whenever
$B^*\ge -R_0^*/4$, a condition both satisfy.  Their negative
$\lambda^*$ values are tabulated as signed Yukawa-branch diagnostics
rather than physical screening lengths.  The second root of the same
quadratic is tabulated as $\lambda^*_-$ for every species.  On the
anti-screening branch it also lies in the admissible interval
$(-R_0^*,0)$.  On the screening branch it lies below $-R_0^*$,
outside the admissible interval, and is reported for completeness.

\paragraph*{Crystal-chemical anchoring of the second root.}
Because the two roots of the inversion quadratic sum to $-R_0^*$, the
magnitude of the second root on the screening branch is
$|\lambda^*_-|=R_0^*+\lambda^*$, the characteristic shell radius
extended by one screening length.
Figure~\ref{fig:lambda_minus_radius} compares this quantity with an
independent crystal-chemical scale.  Each species is assigned a
population-weighted Shannon crystal radius, obtained by interpolating
the Shannon crystal radii \cite{Shannon1976} linearly in coordination
number and averaging over the coordination numbers observed for that
species in the fitted corpus, weighted by fitted structure counts.
The high-spin radius is used where both spin states are tabulated.
Unit-slope fits $|\lambda^*_-|=r_{\mathrm{cr}}+c(z)$ describe each
formal-charge class with $0.05$--$0.13$~\AA{} rms scatter, and the
fitted offsets follow $c(z)=1.05+0.092\,z$~\AA{} ($r=0.97$, inset).
The constant term is an oxygen-radius-scale length, and the increment
raises the effective cation--oxygen contact distance by about
$0.09$~\AA{} per unit of formal charge.  The figure shows the 96
cationic screening-branch species with tabulated Shannon radii.
H$^{+}$, P$^{4+}$, Co$^{+}$, and Sn$^{2+}$ lack entries at any
coordination number, and Cl$^{-}$ and the two anti-screening species
are excluded.
